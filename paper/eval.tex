The system was driven on 2026-09-08 in Westfield, New Jersey, on a OnePlus Nord
N10. Table~\ref{tab:corpus} is the whole corpus. Distance is reported as a
bracket rather than a number. Summing great-circle steps between fixes less than
3\,s apart gives 86.55\,km and omits distance covered during gaps. Integrating
the reported speed gives 90.06\,km and counts the gaps but trusts the speed
field. Quoting either figure alone would overstate the precision.

\noindent\textbf{The shakedown drive.}
The first drive ran 1{,}632 ticks over 279.5\,s and 6.24\,km, at a mean speed of
22.9\,m/s. The mean tick rate was 4.69\,Hz with a median spacing of 203\,ms, and
two gaps longer than 2\,s totalled 5\,s. Distance agrees to 0.01\,km between
integrating the reported speed and the great-circle path through the fixes,
which is two independent routes to one number.

The drive also produced three defects. The camera frames arrived rotated by
ninety degrees. The frames were being discarded, so the drive could not explain
itself. The severe thermal tier ended a session rather than lowering rates. These 
were fixed live using the laptop as shown in Figure\ref{fig:architecture}.

\noindent\textbf{Latency.}
Table~\ref{tab:latency} gives the two measurements. The on-Jetson path is a
bench measurement on pre-stamped frames, so it excludes capture wait. The
phone-to-Jetson figure is the pooled 95th percentile over three bench runs of
180\,s each. Over USB it is 116.19\,ms against a 200\,ms target, and over the
Tailscale network it is 215.63\,ms. The largest and smallest of the three USB
runs differ by 48.80\,ms at the 95th percentile, and that difference has one
primary source. The phone-side send-queue stage differs between the same pair by
46.40\,ms, while every other stage differs by less than 2\,ms. The spread is phone-side
queueing rather than the link.
The wire hop is reported as an order rather than a duration on purpose. Its two
endpoints are stamped on different devices, and therefore on different clocks.
Measuring it means converting between them, and every such conversion carries an
error bound of about half the minimum round-trip time. Its median is 1.97, 2.16
and 2.03\,ms across the three runs, against conversion
bounds of 1.95, 1.86 and 2.06\,ms for the same runs. The quantity is on the same order of
the error bar on the clock conversion used to measure it, and hence is not separable from
that bound.

\noindent\textbf{Every drive reproduces its own recorded decisions.}
A replay tool re-runs each logged tick's inputs through the incumbent sensing
scheduler and requires the recorded decision back. All eight drives pass: 0 of
22{,}929 ticks mismatched, with the success exit code on every run. That
result was bounded by two controls rather than quoted on its own. Forcing one
decision input to a value outside its observed range, against a drive whose
own range is 0.0123 to 0.0246 and whose threshold is 0.15, produces 1{,}632
mismatches of 1{,}632 ticks and the refusal exit code. Rounding the ego speed
to the nearest integer on the same 1{,}632 ticks produces zero mismatches and
the success exit code. The second control reproduces an insensitivity the tool
documents. The claim these runs support is that each log reproduces the
decisions it recorded, and not that the logs are uncorrupted.

The comparison the two live drives were collected for cannot be made from them.
Both live runs report the first live tick at index zero, so neither contains a
segment recorded before the scheduler began acting. A candidate scheduler can
only be scored against such a reference segment, because after the scheduler
acts the trajectory is its own. The six shadow runs are clean references and
contain no live segment. Constructing the comparison needs a drive that runs in
shadow mode for a segment and then goes live, which is a new drive rather than a
re-analysis.

\noindent\textbf{Thermal behaviour is the binding constraint, and it is not
solved.}
The backoff itself works on hardware. Across two 900\,s bench runs the thermal
rule fired on 1{,}504 of 3{,}486 ticks and on 1{,}561 of 3{,}451 ticks. The
recorded cause was a warm skin temperature. The phone delivered 3.000 and
2.998\,Hz against a commanded 3.0\,Hz. Steady state was never reached. Both runs
ended because their clock expired rather than because the handset equilibrated,
and the skin-temperature slope over the final two minutes was $+0.368$ and
$+0.367$\,C/min. Extrapolating that slope, the 45\,C threshold lies about six
minutes past where both runs stopped.

What ends a drive in the field is not the rate. On the first drive the handset
reached the severe thermal tier on a windscreen mount in sunlight, with one zone
at 51.6\,C. The backoff behaved correctly and commanded 1.5\,Hz. What ended the
drive was the platform throttling the application. That stalled the pipeline.
The final tick recorded a link time of 135{,}359\,ms against a median of
52\,ms, and the application then closed the session. Nothing in this system detects or
reports that condition, and no gate reads the link time. The failure was sharp
rather than gradual. Every tick up to 1311 had a normal link time, so 279\,s and
6.24\,km of that drive remain usable and only the last tick does not.

\noindent\textbf{The two clocks agree, and the guarantee states its own error.}
The two devices keep separate monotonic clocks. No conversion in this system
returns a bare number. A converted instant carries its error bound and the
identity of the estimate that produced it, and below the gate it raises rather
than answering with a widened bound. On a loopback null case, where one machine
means the true offset is zero, the offset spread is 12\,microseconds over 145\,s
with a fitted slope of $-0.05$\,ppm. Over a real link for 330\,s there were 357
exchanges, every one matched and none refused, with all nine outcome counters
closing against the pings sent. The minimum round-trip time has a median of
14.8\,ms and the bound a median of 8.0\,ms. Under full sensor load, which is a
10\,Hz camera at 40\,KB per frame and a 50\,Hz inertial unit, 218 exchanges
completed with every channel on cadence. The measured monotonic skew is
$-12.00$\,ppm against the 50\,ppm the design assumes, a factor of 4.2 in hand.

\noindent\textbf{The gate had no evidence on any drive, for a single reason.}
Across the 3{,}913 ticks of the four bench runs, none of the twelve rules had
evidence for its inputs on any tick. Across the 22{,}929 ticks the road drives
recorded, a leader gap exists on 2. On the bench that is a statement about the
rig, which has no rear sensor, no lane detection and no lane-change detector. On
the road it is a statement about one defect. The camera frames arrived rotated, so no drive ever tracked a leader,
and every rule that needs one was unevaluable for that reason alone.

\noindent\textbf{Rebuilt from the same frames, three rules become evaluable.}
The stored frames outlive the defect and the fix landed one day after the
collection, so the observation can be rebuilt offline with the rotation applied
and the post-rotation intrinsics. Over all six drives that recorded video,
18{,}907 ticks, the low-speed obstruction rule becomes evaluable on 17{,}944 of
them, the target-lane front-gap rule on 12{,}718, and the forward
time-to-collision rule on 11{,}781. The other nine remain unevaluable on every
tick. Those three are the ones the structural analysis named in advance, so a
prediction made by construction is now confirmed by measurement. The control is
what makes it a measurement. The same frames through the same code with the
rotation removed give 5 detections in 3{,}421 ticks and no leader gap at all,
which reproduces the drive's own recorded result.

\noindent\textbf{The firing rate is a range rather than a number.}
On the rebuilt observation the target-lane front-gap rule fires on 37.2\% of the
ticks it can evaluate and the forward time-to-collision rule on 40.3\%, with an
emergency override on 4{,}751 of 18{,}907 ticks. Those are upper bounds. The
distance estimator falls back to a width prior when the leader's contact point
is occluded or clipped, and that prior saturates at exactly 2.00\,m for a box
spanning the rotated frame. 1{,}209 of the 12{,}718 leader ticks sit on that
value. Discarding every one of them puts the two rates at 30.6\% and 33.5\%,
which is the other end of the range.

\noindent\textbf{What the rebuild establishes, and what it does not.}
It establishes what the gate could have evaluated on real road inputs, and not
what it did. No drive ran the gate, so every number above is counterfactual and
the scoring tool labels it so. The detections also come from a re-run of the
same detector architecture on a CPU rather than from the device's own engine,
because no weights and no GPU runtime exist off the device. Whether a leader is
tracked at all, which is what the census turns on, is robust to that
substitution. The firing rate turns on the distance value instead, which is why
it is reported as a range.
