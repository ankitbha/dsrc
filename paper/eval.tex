The system was driven on 2026-09-08 in Westfield, New Jersey, on a OnePlus Nord
N10. Table~\ref{tab:corpus} is the whole corpus. Distance is reported as a
bracket rather than a number. Summing great-circle steps between fixes less than
3\,s apart gives 86.55\,km and omits distance covered during gaps. Integrating
the reported speed gives 90.06\,km and counts the gaps but trusts the speed
field. Quoting either figure alone would overstate the precision.

\noindent\textbf{The shakedown drive.}
The first drive ran 1{,}632 ticks over 279.5\,s and 6.24\,km, at a mean speed of
22.9\,m/s. The mean tick rate was 4.69\,Hz with a median spacing of 203\,ms, and
two gaps longer than 2\,s totalled 5\,s. Distance agrees to 0.01\,km between
integrating the reported speed and the great-circle path through the fixes,
which is two independent routes to one number.

The drive also produced three defects. The camera frames arrived rotated by
ninety degrees. The frames were being discarded, so the drive could not explain
itself. The severe thermal tier ended a session rather than lowering rates. These 
were fixed live using the laptop as shown in Figure\ref{fig:architecture}.

\noindent\textbf{Latency.}
Table~\ref{tab:latency} gives the two measurements. The on-Jetson path is a
bench measurement on pre-stamped frames, so it excludes capture wait. The
phone-to-Jetson figure is the pooled 95th percentile over three bench runs of
180\,s each. Over USB it is 116.19\,ms against a 200\,ms target, and over the
Tailscale network it is 215.63\,ms. The largest and smallest of the three USB
runs differ by 48.80\,ms at the 95th percentile, and that difference has one
primary source. The phone-side send-queue stage differs between the same pair by
46.40\,ms, while every other stage differs by less than 2\,ms. The spread is phone-side
queueing rather than the link.
The wire hop is reported as an order rather than a duration on purpose. Its two
endpoints are stamped on different devices, and therefore on different clocks.
Measuring it means converting between them, and every such conversion carries an
error bound of about half the minimum round-trip time. Its median is 1.97, 2.16
and 2.03\,ms across the three runs, against conversion
bounds of 1.95, 1.86 and 2.06\,ms for the same runs. The quantity is on the same order of
the error bar on the clock conversion used to measure it, and hence is not separable from
that bound.

\noindent\textbf{Every drive reproduces its own recorded decisions.}
A replay tool re-runs each logged tick's inputs through the incumbent sensing
scheduler and requires the recorded decision back. All eight drives pass: 0 of
22{,}929 ticks mismatched, with the success exit code on every run. 
The claim these runs support is that each log reproduces the
decisions it recorded, and not that the logs are uncorrupted.

\noindent\textbf{The two clocks, and conversion between them.}
The phone and the Jetson each run their own clock, and the two are not
synchronised. Any interval with one end on each device has to be converted, and
a conversion can be wrong. So no conversion here returns a bare number. It
returns the instant, how far that instant could be off, and which estimate
produced it. An estimate too poor to be useful makes the conversion fail, rather
than return an answer with a margin wide enough to be useless. The margin is
half the fastest round trip the two devices have exchanged, plus whatever they
have drifted since. A round trip cannot reveal how its time divided between the
outbound and the return leg, so half of it is the part that cannot be reasoned
away.

Three measurements bound that machinery. The first is a null case, with both
ends of the exchange on one machine, so the true offset is zero by construction.
The estimate stayed within 12 microseconds over 145\,s and fitted a drift of
$-0.05$\,ppm, so it invents no structure where there is none. The second is a
real link for 330\,s: 357 exchanges, and nine
outcome counters that account for every ping sent. The fastest round trip had a
median of 14.8\,ms and the margin a median of 8.0\,ms. The third is the same
link under full sensor load, a 10\,Hz camera at 40\,KB per frame and a 50\,Hz
inertial unit, where 218 exchanges completed with every channel still on
cadence. Across these runs the two clocks drift apart at 12 parts per million,
against the 50 the design budgets for.

\noindent\textbf{Deployment experiences.}
The rest of this section reports the challenges we faced during our unit deployment. 
They are not incidental to the
contribution, because they are the part of it that cannot be obtained from a
simulator.

\noindent\textbf{Thermal behaviour is the binding constraint.}
The thermal backoff works on hardware. Across two 900\,s bench runs the thermal
rule fired on 1{,}504 of 3{,}486 ticks and on 1{,}561 of 3{,}451 ticks. The
recorded cause was a warm skin temperature. The phone delivered 3.000 and
2.998\,Hz against a commanded 3.0\,Hz.

On the first drive, the handset
reached the severe thermal tier on a windscreen mount in sunlight, with one zone
at 51.6\,C. The backoff behaved correctly and commanded 1.5\,Hz. What ended the
drive was the platform throttling the application and stalling the pipeline.
The final tick recorded a link time of 135{,}359\,ms against a median of
52\,ms, and the application then closed the session. 
Nothing in this system detects or
reports that condition, and no gate reads the link time. The failure was sharp
rather than gradual. Every tick up to 1311 had a normal link time, so 279\,s and
6.24\,km of that drive remain usable and only the last tick does not.

\noindent\textbf{Perception fault.}
The camera frames arrive rotated by ninety degrees. The detector expects an
upright road scene, so on a rotated frame it returns almost nothing. 
Table~\ref{tab:rotation} is the measurement that identified the
cause. The same frames through the same detector give nothing as delivered and
several vehicles once rotated clockwise, and only the clockwise rotation
recovers them. The frame shows a van filling much of the picture with a legible
plate, so this was not a marginal detection.

\noindent\textbf{The vehicle is a hostile environment and the faults are
physical.}
Both devices ran from vehicle power for 152.8 minutes across eight runs with no
power interruption. Two install faults each cost drive time. Cable type is not
interchangeable, because one handset's cable failed in the vehicle, and the
application was moved to a second handset mid-session with the swap recorded
automatically in the run metadata. Direct sunlight on the dashboard heated the
tethering handset enough that its hotspot shut off, which ended a drive. The
mount needs shade and not only a clamp.
