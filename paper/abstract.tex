\begin{abstract}
Traffic jams can emerge rapidly when local disturbances push a road beyond its critical density, making early detection and intervention essential. Preventing such breakdowns without roadside infrastructure or centralized vehicle coordination is challenging: commercial traffic feeds provide network-wide information but are delayed and aggregated, while onboard sensors provide immediate observations over a limited range. We present a decentralized speed advisory system that combines these complementary sources through a global-local architecture. Each equipped vehicle independently computes a network-wide speed plan from a common, versioned traffic feed, enabling consistent advisories without vehicle-to-vehicle communication or a central speed coordinator. An onboard camera estimates local traffic occupancy and applies a bounded correction when the road ahead approaches its critical density. We analyze the timing limitations of delayed traffic observations and the sampling requirements for anticipatory local intervention. We implement the system on approximately US\$500 of commodity hardware and evaluate its sensing, computation, and communication pipeline through public-road drives, using 18,818 recorded frames to construct a simulation camera model. In simulations on the Mainz highway network across five traffic disturbances, the system prevents jams in 55 of the 74 triggered runs that would otherwise jam, reduces average jam time from 130.8 to 3.9 segment-minutes, increases throughput by 3.9\%, and reduces total vehicle time in the network by 12.6\%. These results demonstrate the feasibility of decentralized onboard speed advisory and its potential to mitigate traffic breakdown without dedicated roadside infrastructure.
\end{abstract}
