\begin{abstract}
A jam on a free-flow road network forms when a local disturbance pushes
density past a critical point. Earlier work learned a controller that prevents
this by setting a desired speed per road segment, and measured a throughput
gain for it in simulation. That controller reads state a central observer
collects and delivers its decisions through a cloud service. This paper
reports what has to exist on an ordinary vehicle for the same controller to
run on the vehicle instead, and what happened when that was built and driven.

The rig is an unmodified Android phone and an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano. The
phone samples a camera, GPS, an inertial unit and a traffic API at rates the
Jetson commands. The Jetson runs perception, policy inference and a vendored
safety and etiquette filter. Nothing is connected to the vehicle's controls,
and no advisory was acted on by any driver. Eight drives on one day recorded
22{,}929 ticks over 152.8 minutes on vehicle power. Measured on the bench, the
on-Jetson path has a median of 19.8\,ms, and the phone-to-Jetson path has a
95th percentile of 116.19\,ms against a 200\,ms target. In simulation,
restricting the controller's observation to the fields a traffic API returns
changes throughput by 6\,veh/h, against paired error bars of 44 and 61\,veh/h.
This paper also reports the failures the deployment produced, because they are
the part that cannot be obtained any other way.
\end{abstract}
