\begin{abstract}
Local disturbances like a burst of traffic or a stopped vehicle on a free-flowing highway can rapidly result in a traffic jam. On a road close to its operating capacity, such jams happen faster and more frequently, and take longer to drain. Preventing such jams requires a warning and intervention before they form. In this paper, we design a speed advisory system for tackling sudden traffic jams, where a commercial traffic probe feed and local sensing data is processed using an onboard compute device to identify the early signals of a forming traffic jam, and displays speed advisories to mitigate the situation before a jam fully forms. The traffic feed data is highly valuable for understanding global network dynamics but is aggregated across road segments and lags in time. The local sensing stack is real-time but limited in range. Our two layer architecture is adapted to this case, and on our simulation testbed - the Mainz highway network, prevents jams from forming in 40 of 75 cases, reduces the jam-time from 131.7 segment-minutes to 16.0 segment-minutes, and improves the throughput by 2.5\% as compared to no control. We achieve this with only 1.6\% higher travel time. We built and deployed this system in an actual car using equipment worth less than US\$500, and the recorded data from driving this car was used to inform the simulation testbed. 
\end{abstract}
