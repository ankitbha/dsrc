\subsection{The Global-Local Speed Advisory}
\label{sec:controller}

\noindent\textbf{Global layer: a plan every vehicle computes the same way.} Each vehicle runs the same policy $\pi$ on each published version of the feed, twelve super-segments with five fields each, and obtains one speed fraction per super-segment. We trained $\pi$ in simulation by Q-learning with the action set and reward of a central controller that read the full traffic state~\cite{self_regulating_cars}: the reward pays for speed and penalises density above the critical value, refitted to our vehicles. Because $\pi$ is deterministic, vehicles that read the same version compute the same plan without communicating~\cite{nayyar2013common}. They do not read it at the same moment, because each vehicle queries on its own schedule, so each switches to a version at a time the version itself sets (Algorithm~\ref{alg:agreement}). With $D$ at least the largest delivery lag plus the query interval, every vehicle holds a version before its switch time. Querying at a common second also avoids disagreement, but a version that becomes readable during those requests, or a missed query, splits the vehicles for a minute, and the publication phase moved by 26 to 34\,s in our measurements.
% On the measured Mainz process, a quarter of the vehicles (0.251) would show a different plan from the others if each applied whatever it last received at a 60\,s query interval. By applying this rule, all vehicles achieve perfect coordination.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Version-keyed plan, run on every vehicle}
\label{alg:agreement}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State query the feed every $P_q$ seconds, at the vehicle's own phase
\State on a response stamped $T$ newer than the held one: compute the plan $a=\pi(x)$
\State apply $a$ from $T+D$, or at once if $T+D$ has passed
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\textbf{Local layer: a one-step correction from the camera.} Every 5\,s the local layer estimates the occupancy $\hat k$ of the road ahead, the fraction of lane length that vehicles cover: in its own lane from the gap $s$ to the leader, as $L/(s+L)$ with $L=4.5$\,m, and in each lane beside it from the number $n$ of vehicles seen within the camera's effective range $R$, as $nL/R$; $\hat k$ is the mean over the visible lanes. The layer fits the trend $\dot k$ of $\hat k$ over the last 30\,s and computes the margin to the critical occupancy $k_c$,
\begin{equation}
M=\bigl(k_c-\hat k-\tau\max(\dot k,0)\bigr)/k_c,
\label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
with $k_c=0.178$ and horizon $\tau=10$\,s. When $M\le 0$ the vehicle shows the next lower speed fraction instead of the plan's. The correction holds for at least 30\,s and releases once $M\ge 0.05$ on two consecutive samples, so one noisy frame cannot change the advice. Following Proposition~\ref{prop:margin} (\S\ref{sec:feed}), $\tau$ is the layer's own response time, the sampling interval plus processing and the driver's reaction, rather than a tuned parameter.

\noindent\textbf{When the correction may act.} The camera sees only the next 60\,m, and near capacity ordinary traffic also brings the occupancy ahead close to $k_c$, so a correction without a gate slows vehicles that did not need slowing. A feed gate therefore lets the correction switch on only while the feed version the vehicle applies shows a jam factor of at least $J_g=1$ on some super-segment, measured against one step below that version's plan so that the advisory's own slowing does not read as congestion. Every vehicle that applies the same version sees the same gate, and a corrected vehicle is released through the hysteresis when its gate closes.

\noindent\textbf{Why the correction only lowers, and by one step.} A vehicle that sees an empty road ahead does not know that the network downstream is filling, which the plan does know, so it must not speed up; a vehicle that sees a filling road needs only to slow its own arrival, and one step bounds how far it falls below its neighbours and prevents obstructing the traffic flow from the vehicle itself. Vehicles on a segment thus differ from the shared plan by at most one step, and only where their cameras see the road filling.
