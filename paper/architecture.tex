\section{The System as Built}
\label{sec:architecture}

\noindent\textbf{The rig.} The vehicle side is an ordinary Android phone and a Jetson Orin Nano, with no purpose-built hardware (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}(b)). The phone holds no interpretation logic, as it captures or queries at the rate it is told and forwards raw data. Every association, fusion and sampling decision happens on the Jetson. The phone reaches the internet over 5G. We implement two transports for the same wire protocol, a Tailscale network for development with the phone in hand, and USB in the vehicle. Both devices are powered from the vehicle, so energy is not a binding constraint.

\noindent\textbf{The sensing scheduler.} Each sensor's sampling rate is set at run time by the Jetson and commanded to the phone. The four rates are camera, GPS, inertial unit and traffic feed, and the scheduler trades them against three costs: traffic-API quota, thermal headroom on the phone, and Jetson compute. It has two modes. In shadow mode the decision is recorded and not sent, and in live mode the phone applies it. That distinction is about sensing configuration only. Therefore, the word ``live'' here describes what the sensing configuration did, and not what the vehicle did.


\noindent\textbf{The transport.} One wire protocol carries everything between the phone and the Jetson, over the development link and the in-vehicle link alike. It defines eight channels, each with its own sequence numbers, priority, overflow policy and queue depth, over a shared timebase, and we freeze the frame layout in a single specification file. Neither implementation is tested against the other, and both are tested against that file. Therefore, the two sides cannot drift by agreeing with each other.


\noindent\textbf{The safety gate.} The advisory passes a filter before it is displayed. Because the advisory is a speed, a rule earns its place only by bounding that speed. For this, we have two rules. The first raises a recommendation that would have the vehicle travel slowly on an uncongested road, which is the obstruction case. The second overrides the recommendation when the gap to the vehicle ahead, or the time left to close it, falls below the configured minimum. Each rule records per tick whether it fired, stayed quiet, or could not be evaluated, naming the missing inputs. A rule that cannot be evaluated is inert, and does not change the recommendation.

\noindent\textbf{The device cannot drift from what was trained.} We vendor the observation and action contract onto the device and pin it to a named version of the simulator, so the device encodes an observation and decodes an action exactly as training did, and the export step refuses any dimension mismatch. A second frozen specification file holds the encoded observations, the action schema, the decoders and the neutral fallbacks. We derive every entry twice, once from the pinned simulator and once from the vendored copy, and the generator refuses to write the file on any disagreement. The check that runs on the device needs that file and nothing else. Therefore, the property is testable on the hardware as well.

\noindent\textbf{Two design stances.} We take two stances in our system's design. The first is to degrade rather than stop, because debugging time inside a vehicle is expensive: missing GPS, no camera, no display and no trained checkpoint each have a defined degraded mode. The second is that the hot path holds no queues and the latest value wins, so a frame that was never processed is dropped, a slow display skips ticks, and a stalled card drops log records. For a real-time advisory, stale data is worse than missing data.

\noindent\textbf{Data collection.} A tick is one pass of the Jetson's pipeline. It takes the most recent camera frame with the latest GPS and inertial readings, runs detection, tracking and distance estimation, builds the observation, runs the policy, decodes the advisory, passes it through the safety gate, and writes one record. Camera frames pace the ticks, so the tick rate follows the commanded camera rate. Every per-tick count in this paper refers to that record.
