\section{Limitations and What Remains}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent\textbf{The controller has not yet computed an advisory from live road
inputs.}
This is a gap in the computation and not in the deployment, because no advisory
this project computes is ever acted on. The device-side runtime exists, executes
an exported checkpoint from super-segment features, and reproduces the
simulator's action exactly on the checkpoint's held-out decisions. Its inference
cost is 0.027 to 0.054\,ms at the median on a development machine, and
assembling the segment state costs about 30\,ms at the median on the same
machine. That assembly runs once per 60\,s decision rather than once per tick.
Neither figure has been measured on the target device, and the harness that
would measure them is built.

Three things stand between that runtime and a policy a vehicle could run on a
road it can drive to. The first is a super-segment partition of a drivable
network. The second is a feed query bounded by that network's extent. The third
is a training run on that network. The policy reads the whole network's state
and its weights are trained for one network, so it is network-specific. The
network definition used here also carries no geographic projection, and its
coordinates are a fabricated placement rather than a measurement, so its
super-segments cannot be matched against a query on a real road. The 123 feed
bodies collected on the drives describe New Jersey roads. There is no
observation of the trained network in the corpus and no policy for the roads
that were driven, so the gap cannot be closed by replaying the drives.

Separately, the new runtime is not reachable from the demonstration entry point
as the code stands. The pipeline is constructed without the three optional
arguments that enable it, and the configuration carries no bundle entry. Those
arguments therefore stay unset, and the path does not execute on a drive.

\noindent\textbf{One collection day, one vehicle, one corridor.}
Condition, day and run are confounded in a single sample, and there will not be
a second collection day. Latency and thermal behaviour depend mainly on load and
are the least affected. Advisory bin distribution, advisory churn, safety-layer
intervention counts, and feed-reported speed against experienced speed each get
exactly one observation. A statement of the form ``the advisory changed $N$
times per hour'' describes that drive and carries no interval. The run-to-run
term is not small enough to ignore. Three bench runs of an identical 180\,s
configuration differ by 48.80\,ms between the largest and smallest 95th
percentile, and that is a stationary bench with no traffic and no thermal
excursion. It is a lower bound on how far two drives would differ.

\noindent\textbf{The thermal soak was never run at load.}
The camera held 5.0\,Hz for roughly the first 380\,s of each soak run before the
loop cut it to 3.0\,Hz, so a sustained maximum rate and an engaged backoff
cannot both hold. The GPS and feed rates were 0.0\,Hz throughout, and those two
are exactly the thermally scaled rates. The statement that the handset stays
within limits currently holds only as a statement about a desk. The Jetson's own
temperature is recorded and read by nothing, and there is no Jetson-side
backoff. On a desk it sits at equilibrium with a heatsink and airflow, and a
closed vehicle has neither.

\noindent\textbf{The safety gate cannot bind on this rig.}
Nine of its twelve rules read a field this hardware has no instrument for, and
the remaining three each need a tracked leader vehicle. What would change that
is specific: a rear-facing sensor, lane detection, and a lane-change detector.
Until then the census is the deliverable, and no clamp rate is reportable. The
rate is also not reportable from simulation, because the simulated action
contract has no discrete action for the filter to intercept, so a gated arm and
an ungated arm have nothing to do differently.

\noindent\textbf{The simulation covers one demand level on one network.}
The gain should be swept across demand before it is quoted as a property of the
network. The selected checkpoint is the last training episode, so convergence is
not established. The fleet parameters are the simulator's defaults rather than a
calibration against measured traffic, and the three-into-two exit lane drop is a
modelling choice of ours. The critical density used is 0.178 in occupancy units,
which is 0.308 of the measured jam density on this port and within 3\% of the
published value.

\noindent\textbf{Feed staleness is the unmeasured quantity that matters.}
Decentralized execution reproduces centralized execution by construction, so
that needs no experiment. What does differ on a road is how old the feed's
statement is when the policy reads it. A response carries the time at which it
was received, which this system measures, and it does not carry the time at
which the conditions it describes held. That second lag is minutes, it varies,
and nothing in the response says by how much. This system therefore reports the
first as a number and the second as absent, rather than summing them into one
figure whose dominant term would be a guess. Bounding the policy's sensitivity
to that lag is the next experiment this line of work needs.

\section{Conclusion}

Moving this controller onto a vehicle is a systems problem rather than a control
problem. The observation restriction that makes vehicle-side execution possible
changes throughput by 6\,veh/h, against paired error bars of 44 and
61\,veh/h. On the bench, the compute device's median is 19.8\,ms and the
link's 95th percentile is 116.19\,ms against a 200\,ms target.

Neither of those is where the project's time went. Four defects account for most
of it. A ninety-degree frame rotation hid every perception result. A thermal
sampler was inert on the one device it was written for. A clock validator
cancelled its own signal. A reporting axis could not see an outage that
destroyed its own denominator. Each was found because the system reports absence
with a named reason rather than reporting a zero. That is the transferable part
of this deployment, and adopting it before a collection day costs less than
adopting it afterwards.
