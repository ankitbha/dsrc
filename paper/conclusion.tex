\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A commercial probe feed and a dashboard camera, combined in a global-local advisory, let ordinary vehicles prevent jams that the feed alone reports too late: the feed coordinates every vehicle on one network-wide plan without messages, and the camera lowers one vehicle's speed minutes before the feed's speed would warn. The system runs on US\$500 of commodity hardware, and in simulation it prevented the jam in 50 of 75 triggered runs and cut jam time by 97\% while serving 2.5\% more traffic than no control.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.} No driver acted on an advisory, so the traffic results are simulated, on one network extended by approach roads, and they assume that most vehicles follow: with half of them following, the system left more jam than no control after the largest burst. The approach roads that pace the demand into the network are not measured, so the time vehicles spend slowed before an entrance is not charged to the system; counted, the system's travel time was 1.6\% above no control's. The local layer cannot tell whether a jam would have formed, so with no trigger it reduced throughput by 6.9\%; enabling it only when the feed reports the network near capacity cut that loss to 3.4\% but left more jam after the exit block, which jams faster than the feed reports. The camera's errors were measured on four drives in light traffic without ground truth for distance, so its absolute scale is assumed; at a scale of 0.8 the system left more jam than the lookahead after the largest burst. The feed's delivery process was measured over two sessions on one day, and the jam factor's formula is not public.
